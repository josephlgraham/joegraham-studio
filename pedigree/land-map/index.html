      How this map was made

      Where each layer comes from

      Section lines come from the Bureau of Land Management's national survey layer (CadNSDI), pulled 2 October 2026. They are today's lines, not the 1821 lines. Each forty is drawn by splitting its section into even quarters and sixteenths, the way the original survey laid them out; on the ground a line can sit a few tens of yards off. Sections 17 and 18 are not in that pull: they are extended north from Sections 19 and 20 and are approximate (dashed). Sections 1 to 16 are not worked out and are not drawn.

      Terrain is the 2024 USGS Lidar DEM: 11 County, AL (quality level 1, flown 5 February to 27 March 2024, half-metre cells, heights in metres above NAVD88; downloaded from NOAA's Data Access Viewer). It was moved from Alabama West state plane (EPSG:6356) to web map tiles, shaded as if lit from four directions and tinted by height. Local relief is the ground minus a smoothed copy of itself (about 20 metres across), which brings out small banks, ditches, old roadbeds and house pads. Contours are every 10 feet close in, 20 and then 50 feet further out. Elevations are today's ground. Alignment checked 11 Nov 2026: at close zoom, today's Mount Olive Road (OpenStreetMap) lies on its roadbed in the lidar, and the creeks and contour shapes on the fitted 1906 sheet broadly follow the lidar's valleys and ridges, within the sheet's stated error of about 50 yards. The section lines have no ground feature to check against directly; they come from the same BLM grid used for every forty.

      The 1906 map is the USGS Birmingham Coal District sheet (1:62,500, surveyed about 1904 to 1906), fitted to this map from its own projection. It names the home place Grimestown, with Grahams Mill just southeast and Grahams Chapel to the south. The small black squares are houses. Old datum and survey error mean it can sit up to about 50 yards off. Public domain.

      Roads are from OpenStreetMap and creeks from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset. Roads were pulled only as far east as the middle of Section 27.

      Legal descriptions come from the patent index (all 159 patents) and the tract book, Vol. 427. Where the two disagree, the map follows the patent and the record card says so.

      Census households (1830, 1850, 1860, 1870, 1880) are placed only where the head held land in the same name in this township by that year. That is a name match unless a record ties them. Everyone else is listed in census order, which is not a location. Full table: Grimestown households 1830 to 1880 in the project.

      What is solid and what is not

      Names. Everyone is called by one standard surname (Graham, Gowins, Bolling, Reed, Spradling); the record's own spelling is shown in quotation marks on each card. The place name Grimestown stays as the 1906 map prints it.

      • Documented: a record or the family book says it, and the card names it.
      • Approximate: placed by us, by even division of today's sections, by chain count, or from an old sheet.
      • Unresolved: not known yet.
      • Name match: the same name in two records, not proof of the same person.
      • Family oral history: told in the family, not in a record.
      • The cemetery dot is the GPS point on Rebecca Staggs Graham's Find a Grave memorial (Strongly supported).

      Record disagreements to clean up

      • Thomas's 1854 eighty: the tract-book transcription puts one forty in Section 33; the patent puts both in Section 28.
      • John Graham's 1886 entry: the transcription says W½ NW¼, NE¼ SW¼, SE¼ NW¼; the 1891 patent says W½ SW¼ and NE¼ SW¼. Thomas Parry patented the W½ NW¼ in 1892, so the patent is followed.
      • William's 1871 homestead (W½ NE¼ Sec. 28) takes in the SW¼ NE¼ that Thomas entered in 1854.
      • Ezekiel Staggs: certificate 20995 in the tract book, 20795 in the patent index.
      • The Section 29 patent image is filed as certificate 23825; the patent index says 23835.
      • Three family patents are missing from the tract-book transcription: William 1834 (Sec. 21), William 1858 (Sec. 27 NW¼ NW¼), and Nelley C. Graham (written "Grayham") 1889 (Sec. 20).
      • Thompson's purchase of the Sec. 33 forty from James Gowins: 13 May 1839 (deed vol. 7 p251). The earlier map said 1838.

      The 1821 survey

      The layer drawn from the original 1821 field notes is in Joe's laptop copy only. It stays off this page until the question of written permission from the Alabama Secretary of State is settled.

      Rebuilding this map

      The map is built from data files (parcels, sections, households, places, the 1821 survey, the family book key, eras, layer settings). To add a fact, edit the data file and run the build again. The steps are in the README in Land - general\Graham Land Map - source.
