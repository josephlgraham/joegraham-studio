Farms, 1860 to 1880

What each family had on hand before and after the war, as the farm censuses of 1860, 1870 and 1880 wrote it down, and what Wilson's cavalry took in March 1865, as the Southern Claims files list it. Each family's card puts the years side by side, with the claim between 1860 and 1870. Every figure opens the page it was read from.

"none" means the column is on that year's schedule and was left blank on this line. A dot means that year's schedule did not ask. The 1860 and 1870 crops are for the year before the census (1859 and 1869); livestock were counted on 1 June. The label on each record is for who the farmer is; the figures are read off the page.

  • No 1850 farm census for Jefferson County has been found (Alabama Archives, Montgomery).
  • No Staggs farm appears on the farm census pages we have.
  • Only the family's own lines and the two Belcher lines are here. The other lines on these pages can be added page by page.

Claim 7100, Nancy Ann Graham: allowed in part

Taken by Wilson's cavalry, about 28 March 1865. Claimed $795.50, allowed $555.50. See the page.

  • Mare. Claimed $150, allowed $100.
  • Mule. Claimed $150, allowed $100.
  • Corn: 400 bu. Claimed $400, allowed $300.
  • Bacon: 300 lb. Claimed $60, allowed $40.
  • Fodder: 1,000 bundles. Claimed $20, allowed $10.
  • Oats: 11 bu. Claimed $5.50, allowed $5.50.
  • Saddle and bridle. Claimed $10, allowed $0.

The Commission: The Commissioners found her loyalty fairly proved. On her own showing they thought the corn and bacon somewhat exaggerated; they valued the mare (11 years old) and the mule at $100 each. Allowed $555.50, disallowed $240.

Claim 7099, William Graham: rejected

Taken by Wilson's troops, March 1865. Claimed $672, allowed $0. See the page, schedule.

  • Mare. Claimed $125, allowed $0.
  • Horse. Claimed $125, allowed $0.
  • Corn: 300 bu. Claimed $300, allowed $0.
  • Bacon: 500 lb. Claimed $100, allowed $0.
  • Fodder: 700 bundles. Claimed $14, allowed $0.
  • Saddle. Claimed $8, allowed $0.

The Commission: Rejected. The Commissioners called him "a farmer of means," were not satisfied as to his loyalty, and found no proof of the taking except some of the corn and one horse, on his own testimony.

Claim 7105, Thomas James: allowed in full

Taken by Wilson's command, 27 or 28 March 1865. Claimed $444, allowed $444. See the page, petition.

  • Mare (roan). Claimed $100, allowed $100.
  • Corn: 300 bu. Claimed $300, allowed $300.
  • Bacon: 200 lb. Claimed $40, allowed $40.
  • Fodder: 200 bundles. Claimed $4, allowed $4.

The Commission: Allowed in full. The Commissioners noted he was over 83, a War of 1812 pensioner, and had harbored Union men; three sons were in the Confederate army, two of them conscripted and deserted.
