Farm records 4

What Wilson's cavalry took in March 1865, as each family's Southern Claims file lists it. The Commissioners of Claims decided each claim in the 1870s.

Every figure opens the page it was read from. A figure the page leaves unclear is given as written. All farm records in one table, to search and sort.

Nancy Ann James Graham

Written "Nancy Ann Graham". Southern Claims 7100, File page 2 (summary sheet). Read it: Summary sheet. Documented

Nancy Ann (James) Graham, widow of Thomas.

Life sheet: Nancy Ann James Graham.

Allowed in part. Taken by Wilson's cavalry, about 28 March 1865. Claimed $795.50, allowed $555.50.

  • Mare. Claimed $150, allowed $100.
  • Mule. Claimed $150, allowed $100.
  • Corn: 400 bu. Claimed $400, allowed $300.
  • Bacon: 300 lb. Claimed $60, allowed $40.
  • Fodder: 1,000 bundles. Claimed $20, allowed $10.
  • Oats: 11 bu. Claimed $5.50, allowed $5.50.
  • Saddle and bridle. Claimed $10, allowed $0.

The Commission: The Commissioners found her loyalty fairly proved. On her own showing they thought the corn and bacon somewhat exaggerated; they valued the mare (11 years old) and the mule at $100 each. Allowed $555.50, disallowed $240.

Her testimony also names chickens, ducks, and a fat hog taken; these are not charged in the claim. Summary sheet checked 3 Oct and 12 Nov 2026.

William Graham

Written "William Graham". Southern Claims 7099, Image 00483 (summary). Read it: Summary, Schedule. Documented

William Graham, 68 in July 1872.

Life sheet: William Graham.

Rejected. Taken by Wilson's troops, March 1865. Claimed $672, allowed $0.

  • Mare. Claimed $125, allowed $0.
  • Horse. Claimed $125, allowed $0.
  • Corn: 300 bu. Claimed $300, allowed $0.
  • Bacon: 500 lb. Claimed $100, allowed $0.
  • Fodder: 700 bundles. Claimed $14, allowed $0.
  • Saddle. Claimed $8, allowed $0.

The Commission: Rejected. The Commissioners called him "a farmer of means," were not satisfied as to his loyalty, and found no proof of the taking except some of the corn and one horse, on his own testimony.

In his testimony he puts the fodder at about 800 bundles; Rebecca and Penelope Graham also saw oats taken, and Penelope a hog. None of these change the claim's list. Summary checked 12 Nov 2026.

The same family in other eras: 1860, 1870, 1880.

Priscilla Adams Graham

Written "Priscilla Graham". Southern Claims 9849, Image 00429 (summary). Read it: Summary. Documented

Priscilla Graham, widow of John Graham (William's son); about 41 in July 1872.

Life sheet: Priscilla Adams Graham.

Rejected. Taken 30 March 1865. Claimed $205, allowed $0.

  • Mare. Claimed $150, allowed $0.
  • Corn: 15 bu. Claimed $15, allowed $0.
  • Bacon: 200 lb. Claimed $40, allowed $0.

The Commission: Rejected: "We do not find loyalty proved." Her two witnesses were her father-in-law and J. W. Rowe; the Commissioners wished for other and better testimony.

She said she had just bought the 15 bushels of corn, and had killed five hogs that winter. No farm census line for her has been found. Summary checked 12 Nov 2026.

Thomas James

Written "Thomas James". Southern Claims 7105, Image 00065 (summary). Read it: Summary, Petition. Documented

Thomas James Sr., Nancy's father, over 83 when he testified in 1877.

Life sheet: Thomas James.

Allowed in full. Taken by Wilson's command, 27 or 28 March 1865. Claimed $444, allowed $444.

  • Mare (roan). Claimed $100, allowed $100.
  • Corn: 300 bu. Claimed $300, allowed $300.
  • Bacon: 200 lb. Claimed $40, allowed $40.
  • Fodder: 200 bundles. Claimed $4, allowed $4.

The Commission: Allowed in full. The Commissioners noted he was over 83, a War of 1812 pensioner, and had harbored Union men; three sons were in the Confederate army, two of them conscripted and deserted.

He testified that he owned about 400 acres, had about 50 cleared, and had about 40 acres in corn in 1864. Paid in 1878. That night the troops camped near William Graham's, about 2 miles from his house. Summary checked 12 Nov 2026.

The same family in other eras: 1860, 1870.
